# Conversion Copywriting

Build copy from evidence, not empty-page instinct. Treat every draft as a message hypothesis until real behavior supports it.

This skill synthesizes durable decisions from 67 public Copyhackers articles, tutorials, and resource pages across nine practical areas. Read [references/source-map.md](references/source-map.md) for the inspected source set. The guidance is independently written. Do not copy article prose, jokes, examples, formulas, or voice.

## Hard Copy Rules

Apply these rules to visible copy, headings, titles, subject lines, preview text, buttons, forms, and metadata.

- Aim for Flesch Kincaid grade 6. Treat grade 6 as the ceiling.
- Never use an em dash.
- Never use a colon.
- Keep one defined reader and one primary action per focused asset.
- Use only checked facts and approved public evidence.
- Do not invent pain, intent, urgency, scarcity, praise, scale, revenue, results, or guarantees.
- Do not use names, logos, quotes, metrics, or private details without permission.
- Match the promise before the click, in the copy, and after the click.
- Keep claims as narrow as their evidence.
- Write in the owner's real point of view. Do not use "we" for solo work.

## Start With the Decision

Before research or writing, define these points.

- Asset and channel
- One reader
- Reader's likely awareness and intent
- Traffic or trigger source
- Decision the copy must help them make
- One primary action
- Offer and real post-action experience
- Known proof and permission state
- Unknowns that could change the message
- Baseline and validation method

If the action, offer, reader, or evidence is unknown, produce a research plan or provisional message map. Do not hide missing strategy inside polished prose.

## Research the Reader

Read the target project's source, current copy, offer rules, case studies, support evidence, analytics notes, and prior tests before asking for facts already present.

Use more than one kind of evidence when possible.

- Interviews explain a real past choice.
- Surveys test patterns across more people.
- Reviews and public discussions show natural words and alternatives.
- Support, sales, and inquiry notes show doubts near the decision.
- Analytics, clicks, scrolls, and funnels show behavior.
- Competitor review shows category patterns and open space. Never copy competitors.

Ask about past events, not ideal future behavior. Keep questions neutral. Separate a quote, an observation, and your own inference. Mark each finding as fact, pattern, hypothesis, unknown, or blocked.

## Build the Message System

Create a compact evidence packet before drafting.

- Trigger and current situation
- Progress the reader wants
- Alternatives, including doing nothing
- Main problem and real stakes
- Desired outcome
- Objections and anxieties
- Most valued capability
- Meaningful difference
- Mechanism or reason to believe
- Claim-to-proof pairs
- Offer terms and risk
- Primary action

Turn the packet into a message hierarchy.

1. Match the reader's current concern or desired progress.
2. Make the value clear in plain words.
3. Show why this path fits better than real alternatives.
4. Explain the mechanism or key choices.
5. Support each material claim with nearby proof.
6. Answer the doubts that block action.
7. Present the offer and one clear next step.

Do not blend several audiences into one vague promise. Create variants when segments need different outcomes or proof.

## Match Awareness

Use traffic, trigger, query, and research to estimate what the reader knows.

- A ready reader needs the offer, terms, risk, and action.
- A product-aware reader needs fit, difference, proof, and objections.
- A solution-aware reader needs a case for this path.
- A problem-aware reader needs the problem and stakes made clear before the offer.
- An unaware reader needs useful context before any sales claim.

Awareness is a context hypothesis, not a permanent label. Keep a direct path visible for ready readers on mixed-awareness pages.

Use emotion only when research proves the stakes. Never add shame, fear, guilt, or fake urgency to force action.

## Draft the Asset

### Headlines and heroes

Generate several distinct message directions before polishing wording. Prefer clarity, reader relevance, specific value, and supportable detail. Use formulas only to explore options. Check the headline, subhead, proof, and CTA as one system.

### Pages

Map the argument from the reader's starting belief to the primary action. Set length from awareness, risk, complexity, objections, and proof. Do not use long or short copy by rule.

Keep every section only when it does one of these jobs.

- Builds desire for a real outcome
- Explains fit or difference
- Reduces a known doubt
- Shows how the offer works
- Supplies relevant proof
- Makes the next step clear

### Offers and CTAs

Fix a weak or unclear offer before polishing the button. State what happens after the click. Make price, terms, limits, support, cancellation, and real deadlines clear. Repeat the same primary CTA at points where the reader may be ready.

Never create fake scarcity, savings, deadlines, guarantees, or social pressure.

### Proof

Place proof beside the claim or doubt it answers. Prefer specific, attributable, approved evidence from a reader-relevant situation. Narrow the claim when proof is weak. Testimonials cannot repair a broken product or false promise.

### Email

Give each email one job and one next action. Map triggers, delays, branches, conversion exits, suppression, and dead ends before drafting. Move the reader forward instead of sending the same pitch again.

Match the subject line, body, CTA, destination, and product state. Fix broken checkout or onboarding before adding more email pressure.

## Edit in Layers

Edit the full path, not one line in isolation.

1. Fact and permission check
2. Reader and awareness match
3. Message order and continuity
4. Offer, proof, and objection fit
5. Clarity and reading grade
6. Voice and natural rhythm
7. CTA and post-click match
8. Accessibility and scan quality
9. AI-slop sweep

Remove empty phrases, generic benefits, fake warmth, forced groups of three, dramatic reveals, and staged contrasts. Avoid words such as delve, unlock, seamless, robust, transformative, revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changer, landscape, journey, testament, world-class, and visionary.

Prefer a concrete noun, plain verb, checked detail, and honest limit.

## Validate Before Testing

Use the smallest evidence method that can answer the question.

- Editorial review checks facts, permissions, clarity, and policy.
- Short exposure checks first impression and recall.
- User research checks comprehension and friction.
- Behavior data checks what readers do.
- A/B testing checks a causal treatment when traffic and tracking are adequate.

Define the hypothesis, audience, primary metric, guardrails, duration, and stopping rule before a test. Test a meaningful message difference, not random page parts. Do not call an early fluctuation or an inconclusive result a winner.

If traffic is too low, use interviews, comprehension tests, and observed behavior. Do not pretend they prove conversion lift.

## Quality Gate

Run the checker from this skill directory against every final visible-copy asset.

```bash
node scripts/check-copy.mjs path/to/draft.md
```

Resolve every grade, em dash, colon, and stock-phrase failure. Then complete the manual evidence, permission, message, CTA, and test review.

Report these facts before completion.

- Asset and one reader
- Awareness hypothesis
- Primary action
- Evidence sources used
- Estimated reading grade
- Em dash count of zero
- Colon count of zero
- Unsupported claim count of zero
- Permission gaps of zero
- Validation run and result
- Remaining evidence limits

Do not claim completion when the checker fails, the message rests on guesses, the action is unclear, or evidence cannot support the promise.
