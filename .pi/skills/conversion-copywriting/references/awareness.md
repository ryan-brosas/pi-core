# Awareness Reference

## Job

Match a page or campaign to what a specific visitor already knows, feels, and is prepared to do. The agent should create a truthful progression from recognition to evaluation to one clear next step, using persuasion techniques as aids to comprehension and recall rather than as substitutes for relevance, evidence, or consent.

## Inputs

- Target segment and the current situation that brings the visitor to the asset
- Best-supported awareness level or range
- Traffic source, query, ad, referral context, and promise made before arrival
- Voice-of-customer evidence about pains, emotions, desired progress, alternatives, objections, and language
- Product capabilities, mechanism, benefits, limits, pricing, and availability
- Approved proof, demonstrations, testimonials, endorsements, and claims
- Page goal and one primary action
- Device, format, length, channel, accessibility, and compliance constraints
- Baseline behavior and prior experiments

## Decision Rules

1. **Infer awareness from context and evidence, not demographics.** Search terms, referral source, prior touchpoints, expressed alternatives, and observed questions are stronger signals than age or job title alone.
2. **Use five practical states.** An unaware visitor does not yet recognize the issue. A problem-aware visitor recognizes discomfort but may not understand causes or solution classes. A solution-aware visitor knows the desired result or category. A product-aware visitor knows this offer and alternatives but is assessing fit. A most-aware visitor mainly needs a timely, credible reason and a direct path to act.
3. **Preserve message match.** The landing experience must continue the question, promise, and level of detail established by the source. Do not force cold and warm traffic through identical opening copy when their context differs materially.
4. **For problem-aware visitors, lead with accurate recognition.** Describe the lived situation, consequence, and emotion before emphasizing the product. Be a knowledgeable guide, not the hero of the story.
5. **Distinguish the visible symptom from the deeper driver.** Research both the immediate task and what it threatens or enables. Treat hidden motives as hypotheses until customers support them; never tell people what they secretly feel.
6. **Use emotion proportionately.** Choose emotions already present in the evidence and connect them to a real situation. Do not manufacture fear, shame, grief, urgency, or identity pressure to overpower judgment.
7. **Use AIDA as a sequencing aid, not a mandatory page template.** Attention earns the next moment, interest supplies relevant facts, desire connects those facts to valued progress, and action makes the next step clear. Interest and desire may alternate, and the sequence may repeat for complex products.
8. **Translate features into consequences.** Explain what a capability changes for this visitor and why that change matters. Retain enough factual detail for evaluation.
9. **Ask for one primary action.** Secondary navigation may support comparison or accessibility, but it must not create competing conversion goals.
10. **Use recall devices only with truthful claims.** Rhythm, unusual wording, repetition, and list order can improve salience. They cannot validate a weak argument. Clarity outranks novelty, and repetition must not be used to launder falsehood.
11. **Place essential items deliberately.** Readers often notice beginnings and endings more than middles. Put critical benefits, objections, or instructions where they are likely to be encountered; use visual emphasis carefully when a middle item is essential.
12. **Do not assume a gift creates obligation.** Free content, samples, or trials must provide real value without hidden coercion. Make the requested next step explicit, but do not exploit embarrassment, surveillance, or misleading peer pressure.
13. **Use people and stories only when relevant and authorized.** A character-driven account or demonstration should help the visitor recognize the situation and outcome. Endorsements must be genuine, representative, and properly disclosed.
14. **Treat source case figures as historical observations.** They do not establish expected effects for a new audience, page, or channel.

## Workflow

1. **Define the arrival state.** Record who is arriving, from where, what they have just seen or searched, what they likely know, and what they are trying to resolve.
2. **Assign an awareness hypothesis.** Cite the evidence and confidence level. If traffic spans levels, identify segments or design a path that lets visitors self-select without confusion.
3. **State the page contract.** Write the pre-click promise, the visitor's immediate question, the page's answer, and the one next action.
4. **Map the decision gap.** List what the visitor must understand, believe, feel safe about, and do before taking that action. Remove information suited only to later or earlier stages.
5. **Build the problem model.** Document the triggering situation, external symptom, possible underlying driver, practical consequence, emotional consequence, attempted alternatives, and desired relief. Mark unsupported inferences.
6. **Choose the persuasion sequence.** For a problem-aware page, usually move from recognition to clarification, then credible possibility, mechanism, proof, risk handling, and a proportionate next step. For later stages, shorten recognition and expand fit, comparison, offer, or urgency as evidence warrants.
7. **Draft the opening.** Continue the source message and name the most relevant situation or outcome. Avoid generic benefit claims, premature product detail, and sensational hooks.
8. **Develop interest and desire together.** Pair each important fact or feature with its customer consequence. Use demonstrations, specific scenarios, and proof to make the future state understandable rather than merely exciting.
9. **Add ethical emotional texture.** Use customer-supported language, concrete sensory or situational detail, and an appropriate character perspective. Keep dignity intact and provide enough facts for deliberate choice.
10. **Arrange for comprehension and recall.** Put the strongest supported point early, a clear synthesis and action late, and only lower-priority material in the middle unless it receives meaningful emphasis. Test distinctive wording against clarity and brand fit.
11. **Design the action.** Use one explicit CTA that accurately describes the next step. Reduce uncertainty about what happens after activation. If the commitment is high, offer a proportionate intermediate action rather than disguising the commitment.
12. **Audit reciprocity mechanics.** If giving value before asking, confirm the value is usable, the ask is visible, refusal is easy, and any personalization or social proof is truthful and privacy-safe.
13. **Edit and test.** Check message match, claims, reading order, emotional intensity, CTA clarity, mobile behavior, accessibility, and channel consistency. Define a stage-specific experiment rather than declaring the formula effective in advance.

## Controlled Failures

- **Awareness level is uncertain:** create two explicit hypotheses, segment by source where possible, or use an opening that recognizes the situation before offering paths for education and evaluation.
- **Mixed-intent traffic cannot be segmented:** prioritize the largest or highest-value verified group and provide clear branches that do not compete with the primary action.
- **The pre-click promise does not match the offer:** fix the source message or landing page contract before optimizing copy.
- **No customer evidence supports the proposed emotion:** use neutral situational language and conduct research; do not intensify by imagination.
- **A deeper motive is plausible but unverified:** label it as a test hypothesis and avoid presenting it as the visitor's hidden truth.
- **The page must serve problem-aware and ready buyers:** lead with concise recognition, make the direct action available early, and allow supporting detail to continue below.
- **Several CTAs are required by the product:** designate one primary action and style or place required secondary actions according to their supporting role.
- **A clever phrase harms comprehension or search relevance:** discard it. Memorability is not a reason to make the message harder to understand.
- **Repetition becomes annoying or misleading:** vary the truthful expression, reduce frequency, and strengthen the underlying argument.
- **Fear or sadness overwhelms agency:** reduce intensity, restore practical information and choice, and remove manipulative imagery or language.
- **A free offer does not lead to the next step:** test fit, activation, perceived value, timing, and clarity of the ask. Do not presume ingratitude or add coercion.
- **An endorsement or testimonial cannot be verified:** remove it or hold publication until authorization and substantiation are complete.

## Validation

Before release, confirm that:

- The awareness assignment has cited evidence and a stated confidence level.
- Headline, opening, body, proof, and CTA continue the visitor's arrival context.
- A problem-aware visitor can recognize the situation without already understanding the solution.
- Symptoms, deeper drivers, and emotions are supported by research or clearly marked as hypotheses.
- Every feature is connected to a relevant consequence, and every material claim is substantiated.
- Emotional language is proportionate, dignified, and does not conceal risks or terms.
- The page offers one unmistakable primary action and accurately sets expectations for what follows.
- Critical information is not buried solely in a low-attention middle section.
- Novelty, rhythm, repetition, social proof, stories, and reciprocity improve clarity or recall without deception or coercion.
- The experience remains understandable and actionable across required devices and accessibility modes.
- The test plan identifies traffic segment, awareness hypothesis, baseline, primary metric, guardrails, duration or stopping rule, and interpretation limits.
- No dated conversion result is presented as a universal benchmark, guaranteed outcome, or forecast.
